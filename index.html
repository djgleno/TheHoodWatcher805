Lead story • Paso Robles • Accountability

Reported district credit-card spending raises oversight questions ahead of Measure G-26 vote

A November 1 investigation by Karen Velie at CalCoastNews reviewed purchase information associated with four Paso Robles Joint Unified School District administrators. The outlet identified up to $70,000 in purchases it characterized as questionable between July 2023 and June 2026, citing district card-use rules. Those figures and characterizations are the outlet's reporting, not an independent Hoodwatcher audit or a legal finding.

CalCoastNews reported concerns involving meals, gifts, event expenses and purchases that could require different procurement approval. Whether individual charges served authorized public purposes, were reimbursed, or received appropriate approval has not been independently established by The Hoodwatcher. District administrators should have a full opportunity to provide documentation and responses.

Important distinction: Measure G-26 concerns a proposed $205 million facilities bond on the November 3 ballot. Bond proceeds are legally restricted to qualifying capital improvements and cannot pay routine salaries or operating costs. The reported credit-card questions do not establish that any proposed bond proceeds were misused.

Verification still needed: original card statements and receipts; meeting agendas and attendance records for food expenses; relevant district purchasing policies and exceptions; reimbursements and approvals; any oversight inquiry findings; and written comment from the district and named officials.

Reporting: CalCoastNews, Nov. 1 • Official context: Paso Robles school district bond information • County election materials

E. coli investigation: confirmed and suspected cases

The county's investigation involving Brookshire Farms included five laboratory-confirmed cases and eight additional possible cases in the last available KSBY report. District attorney reviews voter eligibility referrals

CalCoastNews reported October 29 that the San Luis Obispo County District Attorney's Office was examining referrals involving 63 voter registrations flagged by federal authorities.
